We present jmbayes, a data-parallel reimplementation of the R package JMbayes2, a widely used tool for Bayesian joint models of longitudinal and time-to-event data. It fits the same model, with the same priors and the same Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm, and gives empirically equivalent results: the same estimates, within Monte Carlo error. On the largest problem we tested it was 10x faster with 27x less memory, and the gap grew with problem size. For 3,120 patients and 90,000 iterations, JMbayes2 took 104 minutes and 37.8 GB of RAM; jmbayes took 11 minutes and 1.4 GB. jmbayes's memory stayed at 1.3-1.4 GB of RAM and under 0.2 GB of GPU memory in every run, up to 90,000 iterations, while JMbayes2's grew with patients x iterations beyond the capacity of a typical workstation. The gains come from restructuring the computation, not from changing the method: chains and patients are processed as data-parallel tensor operations on a GPU instead of one process per chain, each iteration is replayed as a recorded CUDA graph, and the fit statistics (DIC, WAIC, LPML) are computed exactly from running totals instead of a stored per-patient log-likelihood for every draw. In formal equivalence tests on the primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) data, all 50 model parameters agreed with JMbayes2 within 0.2 posterior standard deviations, and the two programs differed from each other less than two JMbayes2 runs with different random seeds. jmbayes covers JMbayes2's model options (10 marker families, competing risks, multi-state and recurrent events, interval censoring, all functional forms); in 21 further models no parameter was shown to differ, and in 30 repeated runs per program on three data sets every parameter was shown equivalent. jmbayes is open source (GPL-3.0), installable with pip install jmbayes, and available with all scripts at https://github.com/neuratom/jmbayes.
